\chapter{Validation against RSiena}
\label{ch:benchmark}

This chapter compares \nwsaom{} with RSiena on the same models and the same
data, for agreement of the estimates. The runs were made for the Stata Journal
article on \nwsaom{}; the numbers below are taken from it (benchmark scripts:
\texttt{benchmark/} in the article's repository, \texttt{rsiena\_bench.R},
\texttt{nwsaom\_bench.do}, \texttt{make\_tables.py}). Section~\ref{sec:benchextra}
adds models for families that came later: two-mode networks, several networks
(with and without a behavior, and with a non-directed network), and some of the
newer effects. Timings are not part of this manual because they depend on the
machine and on the versions of the programs; current run times against R are
on the speed page of \url{https://nwcommands.org}.

\section{Design}

\nwsaom{} and RSiena \benchrsiena{} (R \benchrver) were run on \benchnmodels{}
models with each program's default algorithm settings. The data of the first
\benchnmain{} models are the 50-actor Glasgow panel (RSiena's \texttt{s50}); the
larger networks are 2, 4 or 10 disconnected copies of it ($n=100$, 200, 500).
Network-only models use conditional estimation (RSiena's default), except N7
(unconditional). Each model was fitted with seeds 1--7 in both programs (seeds
1--5 for the fits above one minute, seeds 1--3 for those above three minutes),
both on a single thread.

Models R1--R5 are applied-analysis specifications: R1 combines \texttt{outdegree},
\texttt{reciprocity}, \texttt{gwesp()}, transitive reciprocated triplets,
square-root indegree and outdegree popularity, \texttt{samex(smoke1)} and
\texttt{egox()}, \texttt{altx()}, \texttt{simx()} on alcohol use; R2 adds alcohol
as co-evolving behavior; R3 is undirected; R4 and R5 repeat R1 and R2 on 200
actors.

\paragraph{A note on the degree effects with a square root.} In RSiena 1.6.6 the
effects \texttt{outPop.c}, \texttt{outPopSqrt}, \texttt{outPopMore},
\texttt{outPopSqrtMore}, \texttt{outPopThreshold}, \texttt{inAct.c} and
\texttt{inActSqrt} had the default internal effect parameter 0. With it the
statistic used the degrees at the start of the period (\texttt{outPopSqrt} then
without the square root) while the ministep used the current degrees; \nwsaom{}
implements the documented definition, which is what RSiena gives with
\texttt{setEffect(..., parameter = 1)} (reported as issue 151 of the RSiena
repository). RSiena 1.6.13 changed the default of these effects to 1 (its
release notes). The benchmark was run with RSiena \benchrsiena{}, in which these
effects have the documented definition by default; models R1 and R2, which
contain \texttt{outPopSqrt}, were fitted again without setting the parameter and
gave the same estimates as the table. Comparisons with older RSiena versions need
the explicit parameter.

\section{Agreement of the estimates}

\input{bench/bench_agree.tex}

Across all \benchnparams{} parameters the mean estimates over seeds differ
by at most \benchmaxdiff{} RSiena standard errors (on average \benchmeandiff{}),
which is the size of RSiena's own variation between seeds (median
\benchseedsd{} standard errors, at most \benchseedsdmax{}). Standard errors,
themselves simulation estimates, agree (as medians over seeds) within
\benchnetsepct\% for the network models N1 to S500; in the co-evolution models
the ratio of the two programs' standard errors ranges from \benchcoevlo{} to
\benchcoevhi{}, and over all \benchnmodels{} models from \benchserlo{} to
\benchserhi{}. Of the \benchfits{} \nwsaom{} fits, \benchnwconv{} converged by
RSiena's criterion (overall maximum convergence ratio below 0.25; the
exceptions are one fit each of R1 and X8); of RSiena's \benchrsfits{} fits,
\benchrsconv{} did. Fits that did not converge are not used in the comparison of
the estimates.

\section{Further models}\label{sec:benchextra}

The same comparison, with the same conventions (default settings of both programs, a fit counts as converged when RSiena's overall convergence ratio is below 0.25), for the later model families: \benchnextra{} models, X1 and X2 for two-mode networks, X3 to X6 for several networks (with and without a behavior, and with a non-directed network) and X7 and X8 for newer single-network effects. The data are the Glasgow friendship panel, a second randomly generated network and the smoking behavior (several networks), and simulated bipartite data (two-mode).

All of them agree with RSiena: the largest difference of a mean estimate, rates included, is \benchxmaxdiff{} RSiena standard errors. Some effect sets were chosen so that RSiena converges; even so, RSiena met the convergence criterion in only one of the seven fits of X1.

\section{Limits of this comparison}

\begin{itemize}
\item One data set (Glasgow and disconnected copies of it) for the one-mode models, and one specification per family for the later models; agreement may differ for other effects and for sparser or denser networks.
\item Both programs at default settings.
\item RSiena \benchrsiena{} (R \benchrver) was the reference version.
\end{itemize}
